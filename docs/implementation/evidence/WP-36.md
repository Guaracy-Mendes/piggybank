## Delivered behavior

1. **Supplement (immutable).** Cutoff, business calendar, T+1 settlement, per-entry limit, return reasons and a return
   window.
2. **Common rules.**
   - Ownership or delegated PAY within its limit, plus account restrictions.
   - The standard action-bound approval challenge, with web or native step-up, before any hold.
3. **Financial keys.**
   - Each entry id keys its hold, its single `BATCH_CREDIT` and its single linked `BATCH_RETURN`.
   - Rejections release holds. Late returns are flagged, never posted.
4. **Files and messages.**
   - Immutable outbound files, sealed at the cutoff by the worker or early by an operator.
   - HMAC-signed network messages with stored replayable responses and conflict on id reuse.
5. **Clients.**
   - Customer portal: **Next-day transfers** in Payments, with recipient masking.
   - Staff portal: **Batch rail**.

Earlier runs, kept here, not hidden:
- **`.local/ci/20261118T191347Z` (resilience drills).** The intermittent native step-up 503 first seen in WP-35
  recurred. With service logs now kept, the root cause was found: a latent WP-27 bug. `identity.native_step_ups`
  set `created_at` and `expires_at` from two separate `clock_timestamp()` calls, against a CHECK of exactly
  three minutes. When the calls landed on different microseconds the CHECK failed, and the step-up returned 503.
  Fixed by deriving both columns from one statement timestamp (`now()`). No migration was needed.
- **`.local/ci/20261118T192331Z` (consumer browser).** The batch journey's hash navigation right after sign-in raced
  the app's post-login return-route handling and landed on the overview. A rerun showed the same race in the ATM
  journey. Both journeys now navigate through the portal's own links, as a customer would; two consecutive full
  suite runs then passed 11 of 11.
